Questions, answered plainly

What are the most common warning signs of a scam?
+

Consumer-protection agencies such as the U.S. FTC and Australia's Scamwatch point to the same core red flags:

  • Urgency and pressure — “act now,” “your account will be blocked today.” Real organizations give you time to verify.
  • Unexpected contact claiming a problem or a prize — suspicious activity, a refund you're owed, or winnings from a draw you never entered.
  • Requests for money or personal information — OTPs, passwords, card numbers, or payment by gift card, wire transfer, or crypto.
  • Links or attachments you didn't ask for — especially shortened or look-alike addresses (paypa1.com, bit.ly).
  • Requests for secrecy — “don't tell the bank” or “keep this between us” is a hallmark of fraud.
  • Too good to be true — guaranteed returns, free money, or a deal that expires in minutes.

One sign alone can be an honest mistake. Several together are the usual shape of a scam — which is why VIGIL correlates evidence instead of judging any single signal.

How do I spot a phishing message or email?
+

Look past the branding — logos are copied in seconds:

  • Check the sender — display names are trivially spoofed; the real address behind the name often reveals a stranger's domain.
  • Watch the story — phishing messages follow a script: a problem with your account, a payment that failed, a login you don't recognize, an invoice you never made.
  • Treat credential asks as hostile — no legitimate service needs your password or OTP from a message you didn't initiate.
  • Beware greetings like “Dear customer” — your bank knows your name.
Does VIGIL visit the links it scans?
+

No. URLs are analyzed as text — structure, domain, encoding tricks — without navigating anywhere. That means zero risk of loading malicious payloads, and it works completely offline.

What exactly is a hidden instruction attack?
+

It is text on a webpage that humans cannot see (invisible fonts, zero-opacity layers) but AI agents can read. It instructs the agent to leak data, click links, or ignore its owner. VIGIL detects the suppression techniques themselves, so it catches novel payloads too.

Does my data leave my device?
+

The rules-based verdict never leaves the machine. The optional explanation layer runs a local Ollama model — also on-device. If the model is missing, VIGIL falls back to a deterministic explanation.
